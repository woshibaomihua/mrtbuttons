How Much Do Garment Buttons Cost?

A useful button quote is not one headline price. It is a price for a defined material, size, finish, logo method, colour-and-size quantity, packing method and delivery basis. This guide shows how to compare those variables without mixing unlike specifications.

Short answer: garment button cost is quoted per piece or per complete set, but the result changes with material, diameter and thickness, surface finish, logo method, colour count, quantity per colour and size, packing and delivery term. For a reliable comparison, send every supplier the same specification and ask them to separate unit price, tooling, samples, packing and freight.

What MOQ means in a garment button quote

MOQ should be stated for the exact combination being ordered. A style split across several colours and sizes may create several production variants, so ask how the supplier counts each variant before comparing minimums.

The five things that actually move your price

  1. Material and construction. Compare resin, natural material and metal only after dimensions, component count and performance needs are fixed.
  2. Plating and finish. Base-metal preparation, plating, antique or matte treatments, enamel fill and corrosion requirements change the process route and quotation.
  3. Logo method. Laser engraving, printing, enamel fill and embossed or debossed tooling use different processes. Ask whether tooling is separate.
  4. Size and thickness. Larger or thicker parts use more material and may require different tooling, packing or attachment hardware.
  5. Quantity per variant. Give the quantity for each colour and size, not only the total order quantity.

Worked quote checklist: a custom-logo shirt button

For an 18L resin shirt button developed to a colour reference with a laser-marked brand, the quotation should make every cost boundary visible:

Product specificationMaterial, 18L diameter, thickness, holes or shank, colour reference and logo artwork
Order splitQuantity for each colour and size, plus expected repeat demand
Development chargesTooling, colour development and sample charges shown separately where required
Bulk quotationUnit price, quote unit, packing, inspection scope, Incoterm and validity period
LogisticsShipment method and freight stated separately unless the delivery term includes them

Changing the same design to zinc alloy, antique plating and a relief logo changes the process, tooling, weight and packing assumptions. It therefore needs a new specification-based quote rather than a material-only multiplier.

Six ways to bring the cost down without cutting quality

  1. Consolidate colours. Fewer colour-and-size variants can simplify production and reduce the combined minimum commitment.
  2. Stay on standard ligne sizes. Non-standard sizes can mean new tooling.
  3. Compare logo routes. Ask whether laser engraving, printing or dedicated relief tooling best fits the material, artwork detail and reorder plan.
  4. Remove finish steps the design does not need. Compare the visual result and required performance before adding plating treatments or enamel fill.
  5. Share the annual plan. Ask whether one order with scheduled releases is available and how storage or packing affects the terms.
  6. Ask which stock construction is closest. A stock reference can reduce development work when its material, size and attachment already fit the garment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum order for custom buttons?

MOQ is confirmed for the exact material, size, colour, finish, logo method and packing route. Ask suppliers to state the MOQ for each colour-and-size combination rather than only a headline minimum.

Does a custom button logo require tooling?

Embossed or debossed logo work may require a dedicated mold. Laser engraving can avoid a molded logo tool on suitable materials. The quotation should show tooling separately and state ownership and reorder conditions.

How are button samples quoted?

Stock reference samples and fully custom samples follow different routes. The supplier should confirm sample type, tooling requirement, sample charge, freight and approval standard before development begins.

Why can metal buttons cost more than resin buttons?

Metal quotes can include the base alloy, casting or stamping, plating, multi-part assembly and logo relief. Resin quotes are driven by resin effect, dimensions, colour development and logo process. Compare the complete specification, not material names alone.

Does a garment button quote include shipping?

Only if the delivery term and freight are stated. Ask for the Incoterm, packing method, shipment weight or volume and destination so product cost and logistics cost are not confused.

How long does button production take?

Lead time is confirmed after material, artwork, tooling, sampling, quantity and current capacity are reviewed. The order should state separate timing for sample approval, bulk production and shipping.
