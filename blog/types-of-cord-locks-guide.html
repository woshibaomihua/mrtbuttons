Types of Cord Locks: 8 Styles for Apparel, Bags and Outdoor Gear

Choose the cord-lock construction before choosing the color. Hole count, cord diameter, spring action and body shape determine whether a stopper works smoothly on a hoodie, backpack, jacket or technical product.

The main types of cord locks are round or barrel locks, double-hole locks, single-hole locks, pig-nose locks, mushroom locks, button locks, bead locks and metal spring locks. The best type depends on cord diameter, whether one or two cord ends pass through the body, the required grip and the finished product.

How to match a cord lock to the cord diameter

Do not choose a lock from its outside dimensions alone. Ask for the usable hole diameter while the spring is compressed, then compare it with the finished cord rather than the raw yarn specification.

  • Send the finished cord: braided and filled cords compress differently from flat elastic or hollow cord.
  • Check one versus two ends: two 4 mm cords do not always fit an 8 mm passage because their cross-sections deform.
  • Test grip and release: the lock should hold under normal tension but still release without excessive finger force.
  • Test the complete assembly: evaluate the stopper with the actual cord, end tip and garment or bag panel.

Selection by finished product

Hoodies and casual apparel

Round, barrel, pig-nose and compact double-hole locks are common starting points. Confirm how the stopper sits against the hood or waist channel and whether one-handed adjustment matters.

Outdoor jackets and technical apparel

Prioritize grip consistency, glove-friendly operation, cord compatibility and exposure to water, abrasion or temperature changes. Test the lock with the complete drawcord assembly.

Backpacks, luggage and bags

Single-hole reinforced bodies, button locks and larger double-hole models can suit thicker cords. Check that the stopper does not damage the cord covering during repeated adjustment.

Childrenswear and drawstring products

Product-specific safety rules may restrict drawstrings or attached components. Confirm the destination market, garment type and buyer protocol before selecting a stopper.

  5. Quantity per style and color, plus target sample and bulk timing.
  6. Destination market and any retailer testing protocol.
  7. Loose parts or a pre-assembled cord, stopper and cord-end set.

Common buyer questions

Is a cord stopper the same as a cord lock?

In apparel and bag sourcing, the terms are usually interchangeable. “Cord lock” often emphasizes the spring-locking function, while “cord stopper” can also include simpler beads and end-stopping shapes.

Which cord lock is best for a hoodie?

A compact round, barrel, pig-nose or double-hole model is a practical starting point. The final choice depends on the hood construction, cord diameter, desired appearance and buyer safety requirements.

Can a supplier select the model from a tech pack?

Yes, but a finished cord sample or accurate cord diameter makes the recommendation more reliable. Include a product photo and mark whether the stopper is visible or hidden in a channel.
